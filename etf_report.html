The rules survive the switch to ETFs. The edge doesn't.

Your stock breakout system, run unchanged over 29 core, thematic and sector ETFs. It cuts the worst drawdown roughly in half — and gives up about 40% of the return doing it.

2019-01-02 → 2026-09-01 7.6 years 29 ETFs · 5 slots 430 entries 54% avg invested 2bps/side · next-open fills

What the test says

risk

It's a risk manager, not an alpha engine. +10.2% a year against +17.3% for simply holding the same 29 ETFs equally weighted — but a -17% worst drawdown against -33%, and it sat 46% in cash on average.

rank

The momentum ranking adds nothing here. Replacing it with a coin flip gives +9.9% a year across 12 random seeds (+8.6% to +11.6%) versus +10.2% for momentum. With 29 candidates and 5 slots there is almost nothing left to rank.

stop

The 1.5-ATR trail is too tight for ETFs. Widening it to 2.0 lifts return to +13.7% and Sharpe to 1.01, the only setting tested that beats buy-and-hold on risk-adjusted terms. ETFs are diversified internally, so a stop calibrated to single-stock noise whipsaws.

Does the ranking do anything?

The stock version scores candidates on 63-day momentum and takes the best. That step only has value when more names break out than there are slots to fill. Across 29 ETFs it rarely does — so momentum ranking and random selection are statistically indistinguishable.

Read these before acting on any of it

The universe is the biggest bias in the test

This list was written in 2026 naming AI, nuclear, quantum, GLP-1 and spot bitcoin as sleeves. Those are known winners; a 2019 version of the list would not have contained them. That's why every comparison here is against an equal-weight hold of the same basket — it carries the identical bias, so the gap between the two is the part attributable to the rules.

Three funds barely exist in the sample

IBIT starts 2024-01, OZEM 2024-05, ARKX 2021-03. Early years trade a materially smaller universe than the headline count suggests.

Costs are optimistic for the thin names

FINX, ESPO, OZEM and FIW all run under roughly $10M a day. Two basis points per side is reasonable for SMH or XLF and not for these at size.

One data repair was needed

A single date (2026-08-28) came back from Yahoo for only 4 of 29 ETFs and is dropped. Left in, it registered as a 60% loss followed by a 150% gain. The mark-to-market in the engine was also fixed to carry the last known price rather than dropping an unpriced position out of equity entirely.

python etf_breakout.py --start 2019-01-01 --variants --robust
